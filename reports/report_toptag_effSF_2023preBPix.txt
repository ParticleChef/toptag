============================================================
2. THREE PROBLEMS WITH THIS FILE, AND HOW EACH IS HANDLED
============================================================
(a) fjpt IS FILLED TWICE PER EVENT.
    For every process in tmcr, fjpt / (class-summed fjpt_cat)
    = 2.0000 for the values AND for the variances. Every data
    bin is even. Other histograms agree with fjpt_cat, not with
    fjpt: tmcr data = 5014 in fjeta, TvsQCD and probT, against
    10028 in fjpt.
    A double fill (same weight, twice) doubles both the sum of
    weights and the sum of squared weights. The fix is therefore
    to divide values AND variances by 2, which the scripts do on
    read (ERAS["2023preBPix"]["fjpt_fills"] = 2).
    toptag/histmaker.py does not fill fjpt at all, so the double
    fill comes from the main hadmonotop processor that made this
    file. Fix it there.
    (Superseded: the scripts now use fjpt_bin. They keep the
    check, and warn if fjpt_bin MC and the class-summed fjpt_cat
    MC disagree by more than 1e-6, so a double fill in fjpt_bin
    would show up.)

(b) DATA IS NOT AVAILABLE IN THE SF pT BINNING.
    Data exists only in fjpt, whose 38 regular bins are
    46.05 GeV wide (250, 296.05, 342.1, ...). They do not line
    up with the fjpt_cat edges 325, 400 or 600 GeV. The only
    shared edges are 250 and 2000 GeV.
    fjpt_bin has the right edges (250/325/400/600/2000), but
    its region axis is EMPTY for every process and for data.
    fjpt_cat has no data (truth matching is undefined for
    data), as in 2022.
    HANDLING: every data-side yield is merged onto the edges
    shared by fjpt and fjpt_cat. For 2022 these are all 5 edges
    (nothing changes); for 2023 there is one bin, 250-2000 GeV.
    alpha, eff_Data and SF are therefore INCLUSIVE in pT for
    2023. eff_MC needs only fjpt_cat and keeps its 4 bins.
    To get pT-binned SFs, reprocess with fjpt_bin filled, or
    with fjpt on the 250/325/400/600/2000 edges.

(c) THE "ttbar CR" CONTAINS ALMOST NO ttbar. See section 5.

============================================================
5. WHY THE TOP-JET MEASUREMENT FAILS — AND A CORRECTION TO
   THE 2022 DIAGNOSIS
============================================================
Process makeup of the tmcr+tecr MC (fjpt_cat, pre-tag):
                   2022preEE   2023preBPix
  W (lnu) + Jets     44.8%        60.0%
  QCD Multijet        (absent)    20.3%
  Z (ll) + Jets      13.1%         6.9%
  ST                 16.3%         4.3%
  WW                 11.3%         5.2%
  TT                 10.6%         1.5%

Truth-class makeup of the same MC, 2023:
  QCD-jet 90.6%, B-jet 8.4%, Top-jet 0.9% (0.4-2.3% per bin);
  classes 7 and 8 (>= 3 quarks) are empty (8e-4 events).

Within the TT sample alone, Top-jet is 35% of jets (31% in
2022). That is a reasonable number, so the classifier does its
job on TT jets. The Top-jet class is tiny because TT is tiny.

This CORRECTS report_toptag_eff_data_toptagTEST.txt section 8,
which pointed at the truth matching (classifiers.py). On both
test files the first suspect is the process mix of the ttbar CRs
— TT normalization in the test sample, or the CR selection — not
the classifier.

Mechanism, as in 2022 section 5:
- The data tag far more often than the MC: tagged fraction in
  tmcr+tecr is 0.248 in data and 0.064 in MC.
- In the raw subtraction the denominator is alpha*N(MC,Top) =
  6.82 * 10.7 = 73 events out of 7942. The whole data excess
  of tagged events lands on this 0.9% residual, giving 20.8.
- In the corrected subtraction the QCD-jet term (90.6% of MC) is
  scaled by SF_QCD = 2.70, which removes more tagged events
  than data has. The numerator goes negative, giving -0.10.

Not a code bug: the same code gives a sensible QCD-jet result
from the same file, where the target class is 97% of the region.

============================================================
6. CAVEATS
============================================================
- DO NOT USE THE TOP-JET eff_Data / SF.
- QCD-jet SF is ONE inclusive bin. It cannot go into correctionlib
  as a pT-binned SF.
- The /2 correction for fjpt is an inference from the file (exact
  2.0000 ratio in values and variances, all-even data), not a
  confirmed fix in the producer. Confirm it in the processor.
- alpha ~ 5-7: MC normalization is still off by a large factor.
- Errors: stat + SF_B + BJetNorm (+ QCDMistag for Top-jet
  corrected) only. No JES, PS, TopPt or scale/PDF systematics,
  as in 2022. Inputs are treated as uncorrelated.
- Test file: do not compare absolute yields with the 2022 files.

Checks:
- The double fill is in EVERY region: fjpt/fjpt_cat = 2.0000 (MC)
  in all 8, and no data bin is odd anywhere.
- Overflow above 2000 GeV is small (MC 0.4-8.5, data 0-8 per
  region). fjpt_cat excludes it too.
- MC stat errors in wmcr, tmcr and gcr come almost entirely from
  QCD Multijet (large per-event weights).

Reading:
- Data/MC depends strongly on the region: 0.6-0.9 in the Z CRs,
  about 2 in sr, 3.6-3.8 in the W CRs, 4.5 in gcr and 6.6-7.2
  in the top CRs. A single global normalization would move every
  region together, so this is process-specific mis-normalization.
- TT is 1.3% (tmcr) and 1.8% (tecr) of the top CRs, and has about
  the same yield in the W CRs (8.2 / 6.0) as in the top CRs
  (9.9 / 7.3). The top CRs are W+jets-dominated (52-75%), and in
  tmcr QCD Multijet adds another 30%.
- The data excess (data - MC) in tmcr/tecr tags at 0.28. TT jets
  in MC tag at 0.44-0.45 there, and W+jets-like jets at ~0.04.
  An excess that were all ttbar would tag at ~0.44, so the
  excess is roughly 60% ttbar-like and 40% W-like. In the W CRs
  the excess tags at 0.14-0.15, about 15% ttbar-like.
  Closing the tmcr gap with TT alone would need TT x433 (x347 in
  tecr), so no single TT factor explains all regions. Both TT and
  W+jets look under-normalized, TT by far the most.
- sr carries data (35644 events). If the analysis is meant to be
  blinded, check that before plotting sr.

Consequence for the SF chain: the ttbar CRs cannot serve as a
Top-jet calibration region on this file. Check the TT (and
W+jets) cross sections / sum-of-weights normalization in the
processor before anything else.
